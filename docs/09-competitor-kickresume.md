# Competitor teardown: Kickresume

Walked 28 Sep 2026, public site and the logged-in dashboard.

`kickresume.com` is **not the same kind of product as Rezz**. It is an
established resume *builder* — 8M+ users, Forbes-cited, 4.7/5 on Google — where
tailoring to a job description is one feature among many. Read it as the
category's incumbent rather than a head-to-head rival; the closer competitor is
JobOwl (`08-competitor-jobowl.md`).

Its value to us is in two things: **the template system**, which is the most
developed in the category, and **the ATS score**, which is the thing
`docs/01-product.md` refuses to ship.

---

# Templates

Templates are the core asset. The pitch is "creative resume templates designed
by professional typographers", and the whole product is built around choosing
one and pouring your content into it.

## The catalogue

**40+ resume templates**, **40+ matching cover-letter templates** (deliberately
paired — "every application feels intentional"), plus **web templates** that
turn a resume into a personal site.

Organised into five browsable categories, which is a useful taxonomy in itself:

| Category | What it means there |
| --- | --- |
| **ATS-Friendly** | Single column, text-dense, restrained ornament |
| **Professional** | Conservative, corporate |
| **Simple** | Minimal, typographic |
| **Creative** | Illustration, colour, decorative geometry |
| **Free** | The unpaid subset |

## Named templates seen

**Main gallery** — Doodle, Nature, Bubbles, Dotts, Blurred, Pipeline, Postcard,
Ribbon, iMessage, Ladder, Polygon, Puddle.

**ATS-friendly** — Double Decker, Black, English, Sharp, Smart, Green, Red, Basic.

Names are evocative rather than descriptive, which is a deliberate merchandising
choice: you pick a personality, not a layout.

## Structural patterns, and what they tell us

Looking at the actual renders rather than the copy, the same devices recur:

- **Two columns dominate** the creative set; the ATS set is single column.
- **A circular photo is standard**, top-left or top-right.
- **European CV fields are first-class** — *Nationality*, *Date of birth*,
  *Address* appear in the header of most templates. That is a Continental
  convention. It is neutral-to-harmful in India and actively discouraged in
  US/UK hiring.
- **Skills as quantities**: percentage rings ("English 100%, Spanish 80%"), dot
  ratings, and horizontal bars. Widely criticised — a proficiency bar asserts a
  precision nobody can justify — and hostile to parsing.
- **Icon-prefixed section headings** throughout.
- **A consistent section vocabulary**: Profile · Work experience · Education ·
  Skills · Strengths · Languages · Certificates · Volunteering · Hobbies ·
  Awards · Training.

### The contradiction in their ATS set

Their ATS-friendly templates still carry **photos and skill bars**. Those are
exactly the two elements most commonly blamed for parse failures. "ATS-friendly"
here means a cleaner single-column layout, not the removal of the risky
elements.

### What this is worth to us

`docs/05-architecture.md` already contemplates offering an **ATS-safe second
download** for resumes whose own design is hostile to parsing. If that is ever
built, this is the reference shape — and the lesson is to go further than they
do: single column, no photo, no proficiency bars, no icons, conventional
section names, real text rather than graphics.

It also sharpens our own position. Everything above is a **template problem** —
it only exists because the product replaces your document. Rezz edits the file
you already have, so none of it applies. That is the whole argument, and seeing
40+ templates makes it concrete: they compete on which costume to put your
history in; we compete on not changing your clothes.

# The ATS score

This is the other reason to record them. Kickresume sells a score, loudly:

> "It calculates your resume's **ATS score** based on more than 20 different
> checks."
> "**The higher your ATS score, the better your chances of an interview.**"
> "Max out your ATS score and befriend the bots."

No applicant tracking system publishes a score, so the second sentence cannot
be verified by them, by us, or by the user. The number is generated by
Kickresume and compared against "thousands of successful CVs in our database" —
which measures similarity to their corpus, not anything an employer's system
will do.

`docs/01-product.md` rules this out and the promise strip says "No fake ATS
score". That line now has a specific, 8-million-user target. Worth keeping the
wording exactly as it is.

Their supporting stat — "ATSs are used by 99% of Fortune 500 companies" — is the
familiar figure that circulates in this category. `docs/07-open-items.md`
already lists unverified competitor claims; this belongs with them.

# Product surface

Far broader than either us or JobOwl:

- Resume builder, cover letter builder, **resignation letter** builder
- **ATS Resume Checker** with score
- **Career Map** — visualised career paths
- **Practice Interview**
- **A job board** inside the dashboard, with live listings
- **Website builder** — resume to personal site in one click
- **LinkedIn import** and **PDF import**
- **Human proofreading** as a paid add-on
- 1,500+ resume examples, a large blog, iOS and Android apps

The AI is stated as **GPT-4.1-powered**.

# Pricing

| Plan | Price | Effective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One month | $9 once | $9/mo |
| Three months | $18 once | $6/mo |
| One year | $48 once | $4/mo |

USD and EUR only. **No rupee pricing anywhere**, and no India localisation. At
roughly ₹4,200/year they are not priced for the Indian market at all — where
JobOwl charges ₹999/month and we charge ₹149 or ₹399 once.

Also notable: they take **payment up front for the whole term** and call it
"$4/month", with a 14-day money-back guarantee. That is the pattern
`docs/01-product.md` rejected when it chose "paid once · does not renew".

# Stack

nginx, server-rendered HTML, only ~7 scripts on the page, Intercom for support,
Google Tag Manager. A traditional monolith — heavier and older than JobOwl's
Remix SPA, and nothing about it is instructive for our architecture.

# What to take from this

1. **The template catalogue is the moat for a builder, and irrelevant to a
   tailor.** Do not be tempted into templates. The moment Rezz ships one, it is
   competing with 40 of theirs and has given up its only real differentiator.
2. **"No fake ATS score" has a named target now.** Keep the promise wording.
3. **If an ATS-safe fallback download is ever built**, model it on their
   ATS-friendly set but strip the photo and the skill bars they left in.
4. **Nobody serious is pricing for India.** Kickresume ignores the market;
   JobOwl charges ₹999/month. A one-time ₹149 pass is uncontested ground.
5. **Adjacent surfaces are where an incumbent expands** — job board, interview
   practice, career map. Useful to know what "complete" looks like, and useful
   to keep refusing until the core is right.

# Editor walkthrough (28 Sep 2026, logged-in dashboard, own account)

Walked the actual editor end to end via screenshots (a real resume imported,
then Templates, Layout, Fill In, Design, and the left-nav account structure)
rather than the marketing site. Recorded here because it clarifies *why* they
need a template system at all, which sharpens the case for not copying it
wholesale even as Rezz borrows the one idea worth borrowing.

**They decouple content from presentation, on purpose.** Once a resume is
imported, it's parsed into structured fields (`Fill In`: company, title,
location, dates, a rich-text bullet editor per role — this is their equivalent
of Rezz's `Layout.blocks`). That same content is then rendered through two
independent, swappable layers on top: **Templates** (visual skin + colour, each
tagged `ATS` or `PREMIUM`) and **Layout** (section-level arrangement — Split vs
Standard for Experience/Education, Columns/Lines/Stacked for Skills, all
independent of which template is picked). The template system exists *because*
their product is a builder that owns your document's presentation; it is not a
feature that floats free of that choice, which is exactly the trap
`docs/01-product.md` and the section above already warn against replicating in
full.

**Navigation**: left sidebar is `Dashboard → My Documents` (their resume
directory) → opening one drops into `My Resume` with tabs `Fill In / Design /
ATS Checker / Proofread & Translate / Download & Share`. Separately, the
preview pane surfaces `Resume Tailoring` as its own tab, with a tooltip nudging
"Try tailoring your resume to a specific job with AI" — tailoring is a distinct,
per-document action reachable from a saved resume, not folded into the base
editor.

**Import feedback micro-prompt**: immediately after parsing an uploaded resume
into the structured model, they show a lightweight reaction-emoji prompt ("What
did you think of the resume import?") — cheap, well-timed signal collection at
exactly the point parsing quality most matters.

**What Rezz is actually taking from this, for now**: nothing structural. v1
(see `CLAUDE.md`'s dated override) renders every result into **one** fixed
Rezz template — no gallery, no colour picker, no independent layout layer — so
the Templates/Layout split above does not apply. The two ideas worth keeping
for later, once/if a real multi-resume library is built, are the `My Documents`
directory concept and `Resume Tailoring` as a distinct entry point off a saved
document, both already anticipated in Rezz's own designs (the "Your resumes"
nav slot in `design/screens/WebAddJob.dc.html`). This is a narrowing, not a
roadmap toward matching their builder.
